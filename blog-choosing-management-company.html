How to choose a villa management company in Bali: 12 questions to ask

Fees, contracts, reporting and the team on the ground — the questions that separate a great manager from an expensive mistake.

← Back to Blog

By Island Dream Collective · October 2026 · 7 min read

Handing your villa to a management company is one of the biggest decisions you'll make as a Bali owner. The right manager can lift your revenue, protect the building and give you your time back. The wrong one can quietly drain all three — and in Bali, where most owners live overseas, problems often go unnoticed for months.

The good news is that most of the warning signs show up before you sign anything. Here are the twelve questions we'd ask if we were the owner sitting across the table.

Fees and contracts

1. What exactly does the fee cover?

Management fees in Bali are usually a percentage of revenue, but two companies quoting the same percentage can deliver very different value. Ask for a written list of what's included: listing management, pricing, guest communication, check-ins, cleaning coordination, maintenance oversight and reporting. Then ask what costs extra.

2. Is the percentage taken from gross or net revenue?

This one detail can change your income noticeably. A fee on gross revenue is calculated before platform commissions and costs; a fee on net is calculated after some deductions. Neither is wrong, but you need to know which you're comparing — and exactly which deductions are made first.

3. Are there setup, onboarding or marketing fees?

Some managers charge for photography, listing creation or "onboarding" before your villa earns a single booking. Others build this into their percentage. Ask upfront so there are no surprises on your first statement.

4. How long is the contract, and how do I leave?

Long lock-in periods and heavy exit penalties are a red flag. A confident manager doesn't need to trap you. Check the notice period, what happens to future bookings already on the calendar if you leave, and who owns the listings and reviews.

Reporting and money

5. Can I see my bookings and revenue in real time?

A spreadsheet emailed once a month is the bare minimum. Better managers give owners a live dashboard showing every booking, payout and expense as it happens. If a manager hesitates to show you what owners actually see, that tells you something.

6. How are maintenance and repair costs approved and charged?

Ask whether there's a spending limit above which they need your approval, whether costs are passed through at cost or marked up, and whether you'll see receipts. Small undisclosed mark-ups on every repair add up quickly over a year.

7. When and how do I get paid?

Find out how often payouts happen, which currency and account they go to, and how foreign exchange is handled if you're paid outside Indonesia. Clear, predictable payouts are a good sign of a well-run operation.

Operations on the ground

8. Who is actually on the ground near my villa?

Bali traffic is real. A team based an hour away can't respond quickly to a guest locked out at 11pm or a pool pump failing before a check-in. Ask where their staff are based and how fast they can realistically reach your property.

9. How quickly do you respond to guests?

Response time affects both reviews and search ranking on platforms like Airbnb. Ask who answers messages overnight and on public holidays, and what their typical response time is. "We try our best" isn't an answer.

10. How do you set nightly rates?

Static pricing leaves money on the table in peak season and sits empty in quiet months. A good manager adjusts rates for seasons, local events, demand and booking lead time — and can explain their approach in plain language. (We cover this in more detail in why Bali villas leave 30% of revenue on the table.)

Track record

11. Can I see villas you currently manage?

Look at their live listings. Check review scores, how recent the reviews are, what guests complain about and how the manager responds. Reviews are the closest thing you'll get to an independent audit.

12. Can I speak to a current owner?

Any manager confident in their service should be able to connect you with an owner they currently work with. Ask that owner what surprised them, good or bad, after the first six months.

Bigger isn't always better

Large management companies offer scale, but your villa can become one listing among hundreds. Boutique managers with a smaller portfolio can usually offer more attention per property — as long as they have the systems and on-ground team to back it up. The questions above will show you quickly which kind you're talking to.
