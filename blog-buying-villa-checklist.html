Buying a villa in Bali to rent out: 7 checks before you commit

Zoning, leases, permits and real income: the checks that separate a good investment from an expensive mistake.

← Back to Blog

By Island Dream Collective · October 2026 · 7 min read

Buying a villa in Bali to rent out can be a great investment. It can also go badly wrong. Every year, buyers discover after settlement that their villa sits in a zone where rentals aren't allowed, that the lease is shorter than they thought, or that the income they were shown was a projection, not reality.

Almost all of these problems can be found before you buy. Here's what to check, in the order we check it.

1. Is it really for sale, at that price?

Bali listings are often out of date, duplicated across agencies or shown at a price the owner never agreed. Before you invest time and money in checks, confirm with the agent or owner that the villa is genuinely available and at what price.

2. Zoning: can it legally be rented?

This is the check that catches the most buyers out. Bali's regencies have official spatial plans that set what land can be used for. In Badung, which covers Canggu, Seminyak, Kuta, Jimbaran, Uluwatu and Nusa Dua, these are colour coded:

  • Pink: tourism, commercial or mixed use. Villas and rentals are usually allowed.
  • Yellow: housing. Living there is fine, but short-term rental is often limited.
  • Green: farmland or protected land. Building and renting here is a red flag.

A villa that looks perfect can sit in the wrong zone. You can check any villa in Badung for free with our zoning check tool.

3. Freehold or leasehold?

Foreigners can't hold freehold title to land in Indonesia in their own name, so most foreign buyers purchase on a long leasehold. Others use legal structures such as a foreign-owned company (PT PMA). Each route has different costs, rights and obligations. Get independent legal advice on which suits you.

For a leasehold villa, check:

  • How many years remain on the lease, not how many it started with
  • Whether there's a written extension option, and on what terms and price
  • That the person leasing you the land actually owns it
  • What happens to the building when the lease ends

4. The land certificate

The land certificate shows who owns the land and whether there are loans, mortgages or disputes against it. Have a licensed notary check it at the land office. Never rely on a copy the seller gives you.

5. Permits

A villa used for rentals needs its paperwork to match what's actually built:

  • Building permit (PBG, or the older IMB) that matches the size and layout of the villa
  • SLF, the certificate that the building is fit for use
  • A rental or tourism accommodation licence for short-term stays

Extensions and extra bedrooms added without updating permits are common. They can cause problems when you try to license, insure or later sell the villa.

6. A proper site visit

Photos don't show the road access, the neighbour's construction site or the flooding after heavy rain. Someone you trust should visit in person and check:

  • Road access for cars and deliveries
  • Water supply and pressure, and power capacity
  • Signs of flooding, leaks, damp and mould
  • Build quality: cracks, roof condition, pool equipment
  • Noise and what's being built nearby

7. The real income

Sellers and agents often show projected income. Ask instead for real booking statements from Airbnb, Booking.com or the current manager, covering at least a full year. Then compare them with similar villas nearby. Look closely at:

  • Actual occupancy and average nightly rate across the seasons
  • Running costs: staff, utilities, pool, maintenance, management and taxes
  • Review scores, and whether the listing and its reviews transfer to you

Location changes the numbers too. Our comparison of Canggu vs Uluwatu covers how demand differs between two of the most popular villa areas.

Get it checked before you commit

Rules and procedures in Indonesia change, and every villa is different, so always use a licensed notary and get independent legal advice before you sign. But you don't have to do the legwork alone.
