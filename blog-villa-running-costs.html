What does it really cost to run a villa in Bali? Real figures from villas we manage

What a new owner should expect to pay, from the actual books of villas we manage, and what comes off every booking before you are paid.

← Back to Blog

By Island Dream Collective · November 2026 · 6 min read

New owners often ask us the same thing: what will it really cost, and what will I actually keep? The honest answer is "it depends", but that isn't much help. So here are real numbers.

The figures below come from the books of villas we manage in Bali, for August to October 2026. That period includes a quiet September, so the percentages are a little higher than they would be over a busy stretch. Your villa will differ, so treat them as a guide.

What comes off every booking before the owner is paid

Before running costs, a booking passes through four deductions, in this order:

  1. The booking platform's commission. This depends on the platform and on how your listing is set up. On the villas we manage it ranged from about 7% to about 18% of the booking.
  2. Regional accommodation tax. Roughly 10% of rental income. Check the details with your accountant.
  3. The management fee. Ours is a flat 20% of the revenue received after platform commissions. See the worked example.
  4. Running costs. The table below.

Regular running costs: a typical month

Across the period, regular running costs came to about Rp 15 million a month (roughly US$830) per villa. That worked out at about 27% to 36% of booking revenue, depending on the villa. Costs are mostly fixed, so the percentage falls when the villa is busy and rises when it is quiet.

These ranges come from the villas we manage, and a few lines (electricity, internet, water) vary a lot by location. They don't include one-off repairs, tax or the management fee.

One-off repairs and replacements

Villas wear: salt air, sun, guests and tropical weather all take a toll. In the three months we looked at, one-off repairs and replacements ranged from about 10% to 30% of booking revenue, depending on the villa. They don't happen every month, but they do happen, so budget for them rather than treating them as a surprise.

What it adds up to

Put together, an illustration for every US$100 booked:

ItemRoughly
Booking platform commissionUS$7 – 18
Regional accommodation tax (about 10% of what is received)US$8 – 9
Management fee (20% of what is received)US$16 – 19
Regular running costsUS$25 – 35
Left for the owner, before one-off repairs and income taxUS$25 – 35

At the extremes it can be a few dollars either side. That is not a promise of what your villa will make. It is a realistic range, and it is the figure a new owner should plan around, rather than the headline booking revenue.

How to keep your costs down

  • Watch every line. In our owner dashboard you see each cost with its receipt, so nothing is bundled or hidden.
  • Get quotes. Repairs and supplies bought from the first supplier are rarely the cheapest.
  • Keep good staff. A reliable team that knows your villa saves money in avoided mistakes. See how a handover works.
  • Fill the quiet months. The same costs spread across more booked nights is the fastest way to lift what you keep. See our season-by-season guide.

These figures are from our own books for August to October 2026 and are rounded. They are general information, not tax or financial advice; ask your accountant about tax.
