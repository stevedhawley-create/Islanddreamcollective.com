Bali villa rental seasons: a month-by-month guide for owners

Peak, shoulder and low season in Bali — who travels when, and how to price your villa for every month of the year.

← Back to Blog

By Island Dream Collective · October 2026 · 6 min read

Bali is a year-round destination, but demand is far from flat. Knowing how the island's seasons work — and who is travelling when — is the difference between a villa that books out at strong rates in peak months and one that discounts its way through the year.

Here's how we think about the Bali calendar from an owner's point of view.

The two seasons: dry and wet

Bali has a tropical climate with two broad seasons. The dry season runs roughly from April to October, with lower humidity, sunny days and the best surf on the west coast. The wet season runs roughly from November to March, bringing heavier afternoon and evening downpours, higher humidity and greener landscapes.

The wet season isn't a write-off. Rain often comes in bursts, and many travellers actively prefer the quieter, cheaper months. But it does shape demand — and it's when maintenance issues like roof leaks, drainage and mould show up.

A month-by-month view

January – February

Mixed. Early January carries the tail of the New Year peak. Then demand eases as northern-hemisphere holidays end. Lunar New Year can bring a lift in travellers from across Asia. Expect lower rates mid-season and focus on last-minute bookings and longer stays.

March

Quieter, with a unique holiday. Nyepi, the Balinese Day of Silence, usually falls in March. For 24 hours the island shuts down — including the airport — and guests must stay inside their accommodation. Brief guests in advance; for many, it becomes a highlight of their trip.

April – June

Shoulder season — and an opportunity. The dry season begins, weather is excellent and crowds haven't peaked yet. Australian Easter and school holidays create short demand spikes. Well-priced villas can achieve strong occupancy here before peak rates kick in.

July – August

Peak season. European summer holidays and Australian mid-year school holidays overlap with Bali's best weather. This is when the highest nightly rates are achievable, and when villas tend to book out furthest in advance. Raise minimum stays and avoid discounting.

September – October

Shoulder season. Weather is still dry and the crowds thin out. Australian spring school holidays bring another short spike. Rates can stay healthy, especially for villas with strong reviews.

November – early December

The quietest stretch. The wet season sets in and many travellers wait for the holidays. This is the best window for owner stays, deep cleaning, repairs and refreshing furniture and photography — so your villa is at its best for peak season.

Late December – early January

Peak season. Christmas and New Year are among the busiest and most expensive weeks of the year. Demand is strong regardless of rain. Set minimum stays early, and price for the fact that many guests book this period months ahead.

Other dates that move demand

  • Australian school holidays: Australia is one of Bali's largest visitor markets, so every school holiday period matters. Dates vary by state, so watch the major ones.
  • Indonesian public holidays: Long weekends and the Eid al-Fitr holiday period bring strong domestic travel to Bali.
  • Balinese ceremonies: Galungan and Kuningan come around roughly every 210 days on the Balinese calendar. Streets fill with decorated penjor poles and some local services slow down.
  • Events: Major surf competitions, festivals and conferences can lift demand in specific areas for a few days at a time.
  • For guests: our Bali travel tips on balivillabookings.com cover the best time to visit from a traveller's point of view.

What this means for your pricing

The biggest mistake we see is one nightly rate for the whole year. It means the villa is too cheap in July and over Christmas — booked out months ahead at prices well below what guests would pay — and too expensive in November, sitting empty. A seasonal strategy should:

  • Set distinct rates for peak, shoulder and low seasons, then adjust weekly based on demand
  • Use longer minimum stays in peak periods and shorter ones in quiet months
  • Open the calendar well in advance so you capture early peak-season bookings
  • Offer monthly rates in the low season to fill gaps with longer stays (see our monthly rentals)
  • Schedule owner stays and maintenance for the quietest weeks, not the busiest
