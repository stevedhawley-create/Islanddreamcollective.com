Canggu vs Uluwatu: where should you own a rental villa?

Two of Bali's most popular villa areas, and two very different investments. An honest comparison from a team that manages villas in both.

← Back to Blog

By Island Dream Collective · October 2026 · 6 min read

Canggu and Uluwatu are two of the most popular places in Bali to own a rental villa — and two of the most different. One is a busy, social coastal strip of cafés, coworking spaces and beach clubs. The other is a peninsula of limestone cliffs, surf breaks and quiet ocean-view stays.

We manage villas in both, so we see the differences day to day. Here's an honest comparison for owners and buyers deciding between them.

Canggu: social, busy and in demand year-round

Canggu — including neighbouring Berawa and Pererenan — has grown from a quiet surf village into one of Bali's busiest areas. It attracts a wide range of guests: young travellers, couples, families, and remote workers who stay for weeks or months.

Strengths for owners

  • Broad, steady demand. The mix of short holidays and longer stays keeps calendars fuller through the year, including quieter months.
  • Strong monthly rental market. Remote workers and long-stay visitors make Canggu one of the easiest areas to fill with monthly rentals.
  • Everything nearby. Guests love walking to cafés, restaurants, gyms and the beach — and they rate villas that offer it highly. (See our guest guides to things to do in Canggu and Canggu's best restaurants.)

Things to watch

  • Competition. New villas are being built constantly, so yours needs to stand out on design, photos, reviews and pricing.
  • Traffic and noise. Congestion and construction are part of life. Location within Canggu — a quiet lane versus a main road — makes a big difference to reviews.
  • Zoning. Some land around Canggu sits in agricultural or green zones. Always check zoning and permits before you buy.

Uluwatu: dramatic, quieter and premium

Uluwatu sits on the Bukit Peninsula in Bali's far south, with areas like Pecatu, Bingin, Padang Padang and Nyang Nyang. It's known for clifftop views, white-sand beaches below the cliffs, world-class surf and sunset bars.

Strengths for owners

  • Premium positioning. Couples, honeymooners and guests seeking privacy will pay for views, space and seclusion.
  • Experience-led stays. Guests come for the scenery and the surf rather than nightlife, which tends to suit design-led, high-end villas. (Our guest guides cover things to do in Uluwatu and its best beaches.)
  • Less dense. The area is more spread out, which helps villas feel private and special.

Things to watch

  • Getting around. Guests usually need a scooter or driver, and some beaches involve steep stairs. Set expectations clearly in your listing.
  • More seasonal. Demand leans towards holiday travellers, so peak and low seasons can feel more pronounced than in Canggu. (See our month-by-month guide to Bali's rental seasons.)
  • Infrastructure. Water supply, access roads and building quality vary from property to property. A careful pre-purchase check matters here.

So which is better?

Neither — they suit different villas and different owners. If you want steady occupancy and flexibility between nightly and monthly rentals, Canggu is hard to beat. If you have, or want, a standout property with views and privacy, Uluwatu can command premium rates from guests who'll travel for it.

What matters more than the area is the specific property: its exact location, legal status, build quality and how it's presented and managed. A great villa in either area will outperform an average one in the other.
