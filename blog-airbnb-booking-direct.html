Airbnb vs Booking.com vs direct bookings: the right mix for your Bali villa

Each channel reaches different guests and takes a different cut. Here's how to make all three work together.

← Back to Blog

By Island Dream Collective · October 2026 · 6 min read

Most Bali villas earn their bookings from three places: Airbnb, Booking.com and direct bookings through the owner's or manager's own website. Each reaches different guests, takes a different cut and comes with different rules. Getting the mix right is one of the simplest ways to lift what your villa actually puts in your pocket.

Here's how we think about each channel for a Bali villa.

Airbnb: the default for villa travellers

For many travellers, "booking a villa in Bali" means opening Airbnb. It's especially strong with couples, groups of friends and families from Australia, the US and Europe who want a whole private home.

Strengths

  • Huge reach among exactly the guests who want private villas
  • Reviews and Superhost status build trust quickly
  • Guest identity checks and a clear dispute process

Watch out for

  • Fees. Airbnb takes a service fee from the host, the guest, or both, depending on how your account is set up. The structure has changed over time, so check what you're actually paying.
  • Algorithm pressure. Search ranking rewards fast responses, high review scores and few cancellations. A slow week of replies or a couple of weak reviews can push you down the page.

Booking.com: different guests, more of them

Many owners treat Booking.com as an afterthought. That's a mistake. It reaches a large audience of travellers in Europe and Asia who rarely use Airbnb, and it's strong for shorter stays and last-minute bookings.

Strengths

  • Reaches guests you won't find on Airbnb
  • Strong for filling gaps between longer bookings
  • Good tools for promotions and mobile-only rates

Watch out for

  • Commission. Booking.com charges the property a commission on each stay, and paying for extra visibility programmes raises it.
  • Payments and cancellations. Depending on your settings, guests may pay at the property or book on flexible cancellation terms. Set these up carefully or you'll face no-shows and payment headaches.
  • Hotel-style expectations. Booking.com guests sometimes expect hotel services. Spell out exactly what's included in your listing.

Direct bookings: the highest margin

A direct booking comes through your own website, a repeat guest, a referral or social media, with no platform taking a commission. That makes it the most profitable booking you can get.

Strengths

  • No platform commission on the booking
  • You own the guest relationship, so repeat stays are easier
  • You set your own payment, deposit and cancellation terms

Watch out for

  • Trust. Guests have to trust a site they don't know. Real reviews, secure payment and fast replies matter.
  • Marketing. Direct bookings don't arrive on their own. They come from past guests, search, Instagram and word of mouth, all of which take consistent work.
  • Calendar sync. One double booking can undo a year of goodwill. Your direct calendar must stay synced with every platform.

The right mix

For most Bali villas, the answer isn't one channel. It's all three, working together:

  • Use the platforms to fill the calendar. Airbnb and Booking.com bring a steady flow of new guests and the reviews that build your reputation.
  • Turn those guests into direct bookers. Great stays lead to repeat visits and referrals, and those can come direct next time.
  • Price each channel deliberately. Account for each platform's fees when you set rates, so a booking on any channel earns what it should.
  • Keep one calendar. A channel manager syncs availability and rates everywhere at once.

Over time, the share of direct and repeat bookings should grow. That's one of the clearest signs a villa is being well managed. For more on what holds villa revenue back, read why Bali villas leave 30% of revenue on the table.
